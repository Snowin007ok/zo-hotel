ZO Hotel UI copy style guide

The Zoho UI Best Practices manual says the choice between conventions matters less than making a decision and enforcing it. This page is that decision, written down for one product, and every page in this project follows it.

Language decisions

Decision What we do Why
Capitalization Sentence case everywhere in the markup: headings, labels, buttons, links, table headers. Where a micro-label reads as capitals, CSS is doing it, not the copy. It is the manual's recommendation and the easiest rule to apply consistently. Keeping the source in sentence case also keeps it readable to a screen reader, which announces what is written rather than what is rendered.
All caps Micro-labels only: at most three words, set at 0.75rem or smaller with wide letter-spacing, and used to demarcate or to name a field — the eyebrow Transparency, a form label, a term in a definition list, a step counter. Never on a CTA, and never on anything long enough to be read as a sentence. A considered departure from the manual, which rules all caps out of labels as well as titles. The manual also says it is a set of suggestions rather than a mandate, and that the thing that matters is making a conscious decision and enforcing it. The readability cost it warns about applies to running text; on a two-word field label the cost is negligible, and the small-caps label is what holds this design together across eight pages. The part of the warning that does bite — CTAs, where all caps reads as shouting at the moment of commitment — is kept, and a test enforces it.
Microcopy punctuation Copy attached to a control — field hints, option notes, meta lines, toasts — takes no period on a single simple sentence, and a period once it runs to two sentences. Fragments never take one. Prose in a content section — a benefit, a room detail, a nearby place, a blurb — is punctuated as prose: every sentence ends in a period, including a component holding only one. The manual's rule is written for microcopy, and applying it to marketing prose puts a period on one card in a row of three and not on its neighbours, which reads as a typo rather than a rule. Splitting the two keeps each component internally consistent, which is the manual's actual first principle. The product already obeys this: 25 of 25 prose strings carry a period, and 22 of 26 control strings correctly carry none.
Exclamation points None, anywhere in the product. Banned outright in errors, confirmations, alerts, and CTAs, and this product has no place where the excitement would be sincere.
Tone Casual and specific. We use “you” and “we”, contractions sparingly, and numbers wherever a number exists. A cancellation policy is read by an anxious person. Warmth without specifics reads as evasion.
Voice Plain, and never playful about money. Personality appears in empty states and headings, never in refund or cancellation copy. The manual puts personality in decorative text and keeps it out of error and confirmation messages.
Numbers and currency Indian digit grouping with the rupee symbol and no space: ₹6,999. Ranges use an en dash: 5–7 business days. Consistency in the smallest details is what makes the larger promises credible.
Dates and times “14 Aug 2026” on screen, “14–17 Aug 2026” for a stay, “2:00 pm” in lower case. Unambiguous for an Indian audience, and never the American month-first order.
Second person The guest owns the booking: “your booking”, “your dates”, “your refund”. The whole page argues that the guest is in control, so the grammar should agree.

Buttons and links

  • A button names the action it performs: Move my dates, Change dates, Add requests, Find my booking, Cancel booking.
  • No generic labels. There is no OK, Submit, Proceed, or Continue on its own anywhere in this project.
  • Link text names its destination, so no “Click here” and no bare “Learn more”. Screen-reader users tabbing between links hear where each one goes.
  • One primary button per view. Everything else is an outline or a text button.
  • Destructive actions stay reachable and readable, but never look like the default.

Don't

Cancellation & refund policy. Refunds are processed as per policy. Learn more

  • “As per policy” tells the reader nothing
  • “Learn more” gives no information scent, and none to a screen reader either

Do

Cancellation and refunds. Cancel free until 24 hours before check-in on the Flexible rate. Money reaches you in 5–7 business days. Read the cancellation policy

  • Answers the question in the sentence itself
  • The link says where it goes

Error messages

Every message states the issue and the fix, in that order, in plain language. Inline below the field is the default. We apologise only for system errors.

Never accusatory. “Please enter your last name”, not “You didn't enter your last name”. The subject of the sentence is the field, or the request, never the guest's mistake.

Forms also carry a summary above the fields when a submission fails. Each line links to the field it belongs to, so a keyboard or screen-reader user can jump straight there, and the first invalid field takes focus immediately.

Confirmation messages

Structure, every time:

Header

{Do the thing}? — Cancel booking?

Description

{Do the thing, with the consequence and the numbers} — Cancel your ZO Hotel Mumbai booking for 14–17 Aug 2026? ₹23,517 will be refunded to your original payment method. The room will be released after cancellation.

Buttons

Cancel booking · Keep booking

  • The same verb runs through header, description, and button.
  • No “Are you sure”, and no “Would you like to”.
  • The description carries only what the guest needs to decide: the room, the dates, the money, and the fact it cannot be undone.
  • For a critical, irreversible action we swap the emphasis: the safe choice becomes the filled button on the right, and the destructive one becomes a quiet text button. This is the manual's “more friction” pattern, used on purpose.
  • Where the guest loses the whole amount — a non-refundable Saver booking — a forcing function asks them to type CANCEL. Nowhere else, because friction that is not earned is just an obstacle.

Alerts, banners, and toasts

Kind Modality Persistence Used for Example copy
Success toast Non-modal, top centre Clears itself Reversible successes, with at most one action Dates changed · Undo
Persistent banner Non-modal, in the page Stays until dismissed Anything the guest must not miss: cancellations, refunds, money Booking cancelled — ₹23,517 is on its way to the card ending 4291.
Failure banner Non-modal Stays System failures, sold-out dates Those dates are sold out — try dates on either side.
Confirmation dialog Modal Stays until answered Breaking a habit loop before an irreversible action Cancel booking?
The rule that shapes this table: a temporary alert must never carry critical information. If it disappears after four seconds and the guest missed it, the information is gone — so refunds and cancellations live in banners, never in toasts.

Toast copy follows {thing} {action done}: Dates changed, Requests added, Booking confirmed, Price link sent. No periods, no exclamation points, one action at most.

Empty states

Three jobs, in order: educate, encourage an action, then show a little personality. Headers stay shorter than descriptions, and the header never pretends to be a button.

Don't

Find your booking
No bookings found.

  • Header is worded like an action but is not clickable
  • Explains nothing about why it is empty or what will appear
  • No way forward

Do

No booking loaded yet
Enter the reference from your confirmation email above and the booking appears here, with everything you can do to it: move the dates, add a request, or cancel.

  • Says why it is empty and what will appear
  • Offers the next action as a real button below

Labels, hints, and placeholders

  • Every field has a visible label. No field uses a placeholder as its label.
  • Format and instruction live in a hint below the label, where they stay visible while the guest types: “10 digits, Indian number”, “Six characters and three letters, like ZO-4193-MUM”.
  • Placeholders are used almost nowhere in this project, because the hint pattern replaces every job they were doing.
  • Required fields are marked once, with an asterisk that is hidden from screen readers, and the validation message carries the real meaning.
  • Groups of choices sit in a fieldset with a legend, so the group has a name for assistive tech.

Tooltips

  • Supplemental only. Nothing needed to finish a task is hidden behind a hover.
  • Triggered by a real button with an accessible name, so a keyboard or touch user can open it. Mouse hover, focus, and click all work, and Esc closes it.
  • They have an arrow pointing at what they describe, and we use them consistently rather than on one icon in isolation.
  • Never redundant: a tooltip on a button that already says “Add to cart” is noise.

Before and after: the current ZO site

Every line on the left is live copy from the existing ZO Hotel site. The right column is what this project ships instead, and the reason.

Before After Why it changed
“Refunds are processed as per policy.” “Cancel free until 24 hours before check-in on the Flexible rate. Money reaches you in 5–7 business days, with a reference number by email.” The original sentence is the exact uncertainty that drives cancellations. Specifics replace it.
“Cancellation policies apply.” “Free cancellation until 24 hours before check-in. Two free date changes.” A warning with no content, sitting under a booking button, reads as a trap.
“A comfortable room with modern facilities.” (on all four rooms) “King bed, blackout curtains, desk, 40 sq m.” and three other distinct descriptions. Identical descriptions make a rate card impossible to choose from, and cost nothing to fix.
“Good stay.” — Arjun P. “My daughter's exams moved, so our Goa trip had to shift by a week. Two clicks on the booking page, no fee, same sea-view room at the same price.” — Arjun P., Resort view room, stayed 14–17 Jul 2026 Reviews build trust through specifics. Two words with five stars reads as invented.
“Members get benefits. Sign up today.” “ZO Return Guest: your fourth night is free, and late check-out is confirmed when you book.” “Benefits” is a category, not a reason to join.
“Direct booking available. Book on our website. Easy process.” “Book direct and we match any lower public price, then take another 10% off it.” Three sentences saying the same nothing, replaced with one promise the guest can act on.
“Payments are secure. Your data is private. Rooms are cleaned regularly.” “Refunds are released on the same business day you cancel.” “98% of requests confirmed within two hours.” “Your cancellation deadline is printed on the confirmation.” Unfalsifiable claims do not build trust. Checkable ones do.
“Early checkin — Subject to availability.” “Early check-in — free from 9:00 am when a room is ready, confirmed within two hours.” “Subject to availability” puts the risk on the guest and gives them nothing to plan with. Also, “check-in” is hyphenated.
Six FAQ headings with no answers behind them Six questions answered in full, including what happens if a refund is late. An empty accordion is worse than no accordion: it promises help and withholds it.
“Book Now”, “Book a Room”, “Special Requests” “Book a stay”, “Book a room”, “Special requests” The site mixed title case and sentence case. We picked sentence case and applied it everywhere.
“Your perfect stay awaits at ZO Hotel.” “Plans change. Your booking can too.” The hero should answer the guest's biggest worry, not describe the hotel's self-image.

Words we use and avoid

Use

  • free cancellation — not “flexible terms”
  • move your dates — not “modify reservation”
  • 5–7 business days — not “shortly”
  • we confirm in writing — not “subject to availability”
  • the amount comes back — not “refunds are processed”
  • check-in, check-out — hyphenated as nouns

Avoid

  • Are you sure, Would you like to — word fluff
  • Oops, Uh oh — never near money
  • as per policy, terms apply — evasion
  • Click here, Learn more — no information scent
  • hassle-free, seamless, perfect — unprovable
  • penalty — we do not charge one, so we do not name one
