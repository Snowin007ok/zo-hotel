Room for all

Whoever you bring,there is room.

Most trips are not one adult travelling alone. This page says what a child, an older guest and a disabled guest each get here, what it costs — nothing extra, in almost every case — and where the buildings still fall short.

Travelling with children

Room for four, and a long breakfast.

A family does not want to negotiate at the desk at midnight. Tick Travelling with children when you book and the high chair and the smaller portions are arranged before you arrive, confirmed in writing within two hours.

  • The Family room sleeps four, with two queen beds and floor space for a cot, at both hotels.
  • A cot or an extra bed is a request on any room type, at ₹900 a night.
  • A high chair in either restaurant at no charge, and smaller portions of anything on the menu on request.
  • Both pools are open from 6:00 am to 9:00 pm, with towels and shade at no charge.
  • Breakfast runs until 11:00, because holidays keep their own hours.
  • Vegetarian, non-vegetarian and Jain kitchens, confirmed with your booking.

Travelling with older parents

Fewer stairs, and an earlier room.

Most of what an older guest needs is not equipment. It is a shorter walk from the car, a room that is ready when the train gets in, and somebody carrying the bags.

  • A room on a lower floor near the lift is a request on any room type, at no extra cost.
  • A step-free entrance, and a lift to every floor at both hotels.
  • A grab rail or a bath seat fitted to any room before you arrive, at no charge.
  • Someone will carry the bags from the car to the room.
  • A quieter room away from the pool and the road is a request.

Travelling with a disability

The bathroom, not a brochure.

This is what an accessible bathroom here looks like: a shower you can roll into, a seat that folds down, and rails where you need to put a hand. Access needs differ from one guest to the next, so what follows is a list rather than an adjective.

  • An accessible room costs the standard rate for that room type, never more.
  • A step-free entrance and a lift to every floor at both hotels.
  • Whatever you ask for, confirmed in writing within two hours.

Getting in and around

A step-free entrance, lifts to every floor, and accessible parking beside the door at no charge.

In the room

Wider doorways, grab rails by the bed and the bath, and a roll-in shower with a seat.

Hearing and sight

A hearing loop at reception, a visual fire alarm and a vibrating pillow pad in accessible rooms, and menus and hotel information in large print.

In an accessible room

  • A doorway a wheelchair fits through, and turning space beside the bed.
  • A roll-in shower with a fold-down seat, and grab rails at the shower and the toilet.
  • A bed at transfer height, with the wardrobe rail and the peephole set lower.
  • Light switches and sockets reachable from a seated position.
  • A wheelchair to borrow at either hotel, and a step-free route to the pool and the restaurant.
  • Assistance dogs are welcome in every room and every restaurant, with a relief area outside.

What is not step-free

  • In Goa the garden villas are reached by a stepped path, so we place accessible bookings in the main wing, which has lift access throughout.
  • In Mumbai there is one shallow step at the courtyard pool gate. Tell us and somebody will meet you there with the ramp.
  • Neither hotel has a pool hoist. We will say so again if you ask, rather than letting you find out at the water.

If anything else here would stop you, ask before you book and we will measure it and tell you.

This website

Built to the accessibility guidelines.

This site is built to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1, level AA. In practice that means every page works by keyboard, carries visible focus, and has been measured for contrast rather than assumed to pass. Dialogs trap focus, return it where you left it, and close on Escape. Star ratings and decorative marks are hidden from screen readers, and every photograph carries a description unless it is decoration.
